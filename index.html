Migrating a large, mature Windows-based C++ codebase to Linux while establishing a long-term cross-platform engineering strategy.

Energy-Efficient High-Performance Computing

During my PhD at UMONS, I investigated how computational workloads can be executed more efficiently by modeling and optimizing the relationship between performance, resource utilization, and energy consumption.

My research explored analytical energy models, workload characterization, resource allocation, Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling (DVFS), and Dynamic Power Management (DPM) for high-performance computing environments.

Focus

HPC Performance Modeling Energy Efficiency DVFS DPM Parallel Computing

Parallel Performance Analysis

Research and tooling for understanding the scalability and behavior of parallel applications through performance analysis and hardware performance counters.

This work included automated assessment of parallel scalability, application fingerprinting, modeling, comparison, and visualization.

A Few Things I Care About

A Few Things I Care About

Understand the system.

Abstractions are useful, but understanding what happens underneath them is often essential when reliability and performance matter.

Measure before optimizing.

Performance decisions should be driven by evidence, measurements, and an understanding of system behavior.

Build for the long term.

Software that works today is not necessarily software that can be maintained, evolved, and operated for years.

Bridge research and engineering.

Interesting ideas become valuable when they can survive contact with real systems, real constraints, and real users.
